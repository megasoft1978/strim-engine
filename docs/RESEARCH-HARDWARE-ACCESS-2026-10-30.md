# Free compute and visibility for Strim (research snapshot: 2026-10-30)

Scope: independent Swift/MLX inference project, currently small audience, Mac-first. “Free hardware” is distinct from cloud credits: the best route to high-memory Apple Silicon remains a reviewed loan/demo arrangement; most public grants found are money or non-Apple compute. Product/program availability changes quickly; verify current terms before applying.

## 1. Case studies: how comparable developers got machines

- **Jeff Geerling — Apple loan for RDMA-over-Thunderbolt coverage (strongly sourced, direct precedent).** His Dec. 2025 post says Apple loaned him the Mac Studios for testing the then-new macOS 26.2 feature. He published a reproducible cluster demonstration and named the loan in the post. This is a clear media/research access pattern: a timely, technically demonstrable story about Apple platform capability, with public reach. [Jeff Geerling: 1.5 TB on Mac Studio](https://www.jeffgeerling.com/blog/2025/15-tb-vram-on-mac-studio-rdma-over-thunderbolt-5/); [Apple TN3205](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3205-low-latency-communication-with-rdma-over-thunderbolt).
- **EXO Labs — collaboration/feature visibility, hardware ownership unclear.** EXO’s public work is an open-source heterogeneous inference runtime; it demonstrated combining two NVIDIA DGX Spark systems with an M3 Ultra Mac Studio and was featured around Apple’s clustering/RDMA story. Available reporting does not establish whether EXO owned, borrowed, or was given every unit in that demo; don’t present this as a confirmed donation or loan. [Tom’s Hardware on EXO’s mixed cluster](https://www.tomshardware.com/software/two-nvidia-dgx-spark-systems-combined-with-m3-ultra-mac-studio-to-create-blistering-llm-system-exo-labs-demonstrates-disaggregated-ai-inference-and-achieves-a-2-8-benchmark-boost); [Apple ML research on MLX/RDMA](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/mlx-distributed-inference).
- **Apple’s own MLX/RDMA demonstrations — employer/internal access, not a public seeding program.** Apple’s research post and WWDC material demonstrate four-Mac clusters, MLX distributed inference, and RDMA. Those are evidence Apple invests in this technical narrative, but do not imply that outside maintainers can apply for loaners. [Apple ML research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/mlx-distributed-inference); [WWDC 2026 session](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/233/).
- **antirez / Salvatore Sanfilippo — no sourced loan/donation claim found.** Dwarfstar and his local-inference writing make him a useful solo-developer comparator, but public material located did not document the purchase, employer provision, sponsorship, or loan provenance of his Mac. Treat hardware ownership as unknown. [antirez GitHub](https://github.com/antirez); [Dwarfstar](https://github.com/antirez/dwarfstar).
- **llama.cpp / ggml contributors and MLX community developers — no reliable general hardware-provenance case found.** Public repos/issues show contribution and benchmarking, not who furnished contributors’ machines. Do not infer that Apple or NVIDIA supplied hardware just because a project is popular or appears in a vendor demo. Apple’s MLX developer community and upstream repositories are better understood as technical access/visibility channels than equipment programs. [MLX organization](https://github.com/ml-explore); [mlx-swift](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-swift); [MLX Community](https://mlxcommunity.com/).
- **Jeff Geerling-style reviewers** often receive review units on loan, with explicit acknowledgement. This is press access, not a standing grant open to small OSS maintainers. A practical route is to produce a strong story and offer a concrete, bounded evaluation, rather than ask generically for a machine.

## 2. Programs and channels

### Best fit for Mac-first Strim

1. **Apple contact / MLX engagement (no public general Mac Studio grant located).** Apple offers developer technical support and MLX is actively maintained in public repositories; Apple’s RDMA story is timely. There is no evidence of a standing public Mac hardware seeding application for independent OSS inference engines. Use the public `ml-explore/mlx` and `mlx-swift` issue/PR channels with focused reproducible bug reports and substantive upstream contributions; for hardware access, pitch Apple Developer Relations/MLX authors a specific evaluation package (machine/configuration, test matrix, dates, return/shipping handling, deliverable, disclosure). A press-loan route is more evidenced than a generic developer grant. [Apple Developer support](https://developer.apple.com/support/); [mlx-swift](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-swift); [RDMA tech note](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3205-low-latency-communication-with-rdma-over-thunderbolt).
2. **MacStadium Open Source Program.** Dedicated cloud-hosted bare-metal Mac mini for qualifying free/open-source software in the Apple ecosystem, useful for CI/build/testing and compatibility, not equivalent to a 256–512 GB Ultra or a local Thunderbolt cluster. Apply through the program page with project, open-source status, and macOS CI need; reviewed access, not guaranteed. The public post is old, so confirm model, waitlist, and present terms. [MacStadium program description](https://macstadium.com/blog/macstadium-supports-the-open-source-community).
3. **GreenMini Open Source Program.** Current program page offers a dedicated free Mac mini for active FOSS needing macOS compile/test/CI infrastructure; a mention is appreciated but explicitly optional. It is useful for CI; no high-memory workstation promise. Apply using the page’s form. [GreenMini](https://www.greenmini.nl/open-source-program/).
4. **GitHub Sponsors.** Any OSS contributor in a supported region can apply; contribution includes code, triage, docs, leadership, and project work. Set up a personal sponsored-developer profile, payment/tax details and tiers, enable 2FA, then expose Sponsor buttons. This creates recurring support that can be earmarked transparently for hardware; matching/funding is not guaranteed. [Eligibility and setup](https://docs.github.com/en/sponsors/receiving-sponsorships-through-github-sponsors/about-github-sponsors-for-open-source-contributors).
5. **NLnet.** Technical grants for open-licensed software/hardware/content and open standards; EU-supported calls need a strong European dimension. Strim’s Italy/EU base is a fit geographically, but the proposal must have a public-interest/open-infrastructure outcome, not merely “buy me a Mac.” Application page listed Nov. 3, 2026 noon CET as next deadline at research time; policy explicitly addresses AI-assisted work, so read it closely and submit in your own words. Grants fund technical work, not necessarily hardware purchases. [Apply](https://nlnet.nl/propose/).
6. **Sovereign Tech Fund (Germany).** Funds prevalent, important open digital base technologies; currently says it does not fund prototypes or user-facing applications. A small standalone inference engine likely has weak fit today; a widely adopted low-level Swift/MLX distributed-inference component or critical protocol could fit later. Application-to-contract process is roughly six months. [Criteria/process](https://www.sovereign.tech/programs/fund/).

### Non-Apple compute and funding (useful for portability, lower priority)

- **AMD AI Developer Program / Developer Cloud.** Free membership page advertises $100 AMD Developer Cloud credits, courses, support, and events; resources page describes applying for additional project credits. Apply online, then follow the account/credit activation instructions. Good for ROCm correctness/portability comparisons, not MLX performance or Mac memory behavior. [Program](https://developer.amd.com/ai-developer-program/); [cloud access](https://www.amd.com/en/developer/resources/cloud-access.html).
- **NVIDIA Inception.** Startup program, not an individual OSS hardware grant. Benefits include tools/training, preferred pricing, partner offers, and ecosystem exposure. Requires a startup/project company profile and an application; no guaranteed DGX or GPU award. Makes sense only if Strim has a real cross-platform product/company plan. [Official program](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/startups/).
- **DGX Spark seeding.** No public, general-purpose individual OSS application for free DGX Spark hardware found. Treat press/influencer review access as discretionary outreach, not a program. An NVIDIA-ready port plus measured cross-platform story raises relevance.
- **Hugging Face Community GPU grants / ZeroGPU.** Useful when there is a public demo Space. Community grant applications are posted as a discussion on the Space; grant can attach dedicated GPU capacity for approved public/open research, hobbyist, or educational projects. Free accounts already have two ZeroGPU Spaces; dedicated GPU grant is for cases those cannot handle. A Swift/Metal engine cannot run on HF’s Linux CUDA GPU, so use only for a separate API/demo or comparative baseline. [Community grant instructions](https://github.com/huggingface/skills/blob/main/skills/huggingface-spaces/references/grants.md); [ZeroGPU](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/spaces-zerogpu).
- **Modal.** Starter tier page currently lists $30 monthly included compute; academics can request up to $10k; startups apply separately. It is Python/container-oriented GPU/CPU cloud, unsuitable for running native Swift/Metal but useful for a comparison service or benchmark control. [Pricing](https://modal.com/pricing); [academics](https://modal.com/academics).
- **Vast.ai.** Official startup offer: $2,500 GPU credits for startups moving workloads to Vast; not a general OSS-maintainer offer and requires startup use case. Could support CUDA comparator runs, not Mac tests. [Program](https://vast.ai/article/vast-ai-startup-program).
- **RunPod.** Creator program offers early feature access/credits for a planned article, video, or experiment, rather than an unrestricted OSS compute grant. Apply with a concrete creator deliverable. Useful for cross-platform benchmarks; not Mac hardware. [Creator program](https://www.runpod.io/creator-program).
- **Lambda.** Current documented research offers should be checked directly; secondary listings reported $5,000 research credits in 2026 and a $7,500 Inception/Connect-linked startup offer, but these are eligibility-gated and offer pages can change. Do not count these as assured without direct confirmation. [Research](https://lambda.ai/research); [Inception offer](https://lambda.ai/nvidia-inception).
- **Google Cloud / AWS / Azure.** General startup credits chiefly target eligible startups, often with partner/funding requirements; they are cloud credits, not free physical devices, and CUDA GPU quota/cost constraints still apply. AWS Activate’s current credit levels are stage/partner dependent. Microsoft/GitHub Azure credits are included in selected Secure Open Source Fund cohorts. No broad standalone “open source Mac-first project gets free GPU” offer found. [AWS Activate overview](https://aws.amazon.com/aws-startups/learn/everything-you-need-to-know-about-aws-activate-credits/); [GitHub Secure Open Source Fund](https://github.com/open-source/github-secure-open-source-fund).
- **GitHub Secure Open Source Fund.** Cohort program for maintainers doing security work, not equipment. Official page reports $10,000 award in tranches and Azure credits, plus security education/guidance. Application is for maintainers/projects with adoption and security scope; potentially relevant if Strim has meaningful users and a credible security plan, but low probability at today’s small audience. [Program](https://github.com/open-source/github-secure-open-source-fund).
- **GitHub Accelerator.** Historic rounds are not necessarily open in 2026; no current open application located. Don’t plan around old “$20k accelerator” posts until a live official call appears. [GitHub open source funding hub](https://github.com/open-source/github-fund).
- **Mozilla MOSS.** Official page says indefinite hiatus and not accepting applications; check Mozilla Technology Fund for live calls instead. [MOSS status](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/moss/).

## 3. Visibility tactics for this niche

- **Publish a reproducible benchmark artifact, not a speed claim.** Include exact Mac/chip/RAM, macOS, model/revision/quantization, context, prompt and generation lengths, warm/cold runs, token/s for prefill and decode, memory peak, power if measurable, commit/build flags, and raw JSON/CSV. Separate apples-to-apples model/runtime comparisons from “best configuration” comparisons. A 2026 Tom’s Hardware Mac Studio comparison shows the format: named models/quantization, harness (`llama-benchy`), context depths, and comparisons against GB10/Strix Halo; this is legible to press and upstream maintainers. [Example comparison](https://www.tomshardware.com/desktops/exploring-apple-silicons-local-ai-performance-with-the-mac-studio-and-m4-max-m4-max-beats-gb10-and-strix-halo-in-decode-throughput-but-memory-bandwidth-isnt-everything).
- **Make a time-sensitive systems demo.** RDMA over Thunderbolt has current Apple attention; demonstrate Strim’s Swift/MLX engine across 2–4 nodes, clearly state what works and what does not, and compare single Mac vs cluster with public scripts. Apple’s own MLX post and Jeff Geerling’s loaner story show vendor/media interest in this exact narrative. [Apple](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/mlx-distributed-inference); [Jeff](https://www.jeffgeerling.com/blog/2025/15-tb-vram-on-mac-studio-rdma-over-thunderbolt-5/).
- **Upstream focused PRs to `ml-explore/mlx` and `mlx-swift`.** Small fixes to Swift API, distributed execution, kernels, quantization, examples, or reproducibility have durable credibility and reach the MLX maintainers. Link Strim experiments to minimal upstream issues; avoid asking upstream to endorse Strim. Current mlx-swift release notes visibly credit outside contributors, showing contributor attribution is an active channel. [mlx-swift repo](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-swift); [releases](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-swift/releases).
- **Package the comparison question people already ask.** Publish Strim vs llama.cpp, MLX-LM, and Rapid-Matrix/Rapid-MLX with identical weights, quantization, prompts, context, memory accounting, startup and steady-state throughput. Include command lines and caveats; don’t cherry-pick. Phrase as “where Strim wins/loses” and invite maintainers to correct methods.
- **Launch in communities with a concrete artifact.** Hacker News works best as a “Show HN” for a working demo/repo/blog; r/LocalLLaMA is receptive to detailed local-inference results if the post gives hardware/model/methodology and answers tradeoffs. X is useful for short clips and tagging relevant maintainers/Apple MLX accounts, but not evidence of reliable funding by itself. Avoid generic “please sponsor me” posts. These are tactical recommendations; source evidence for direct hardware grants from posts is sparse.
- **Request review access as a bounded media/engineering project.** After publishing strong results, send a concise one-page brief to Apple Developer Relations, Apple MLX contacts, and hardware vendors: concrete question, why current hardware is necessary, exact configuration, schedule, outputs (benchmark suite/video/article/upstream fixes), audience/reach, return and disclosure terms. Jeff’s documented Apple loan is precedent for a testable story, not a guaranteed application pathway.
- **Use funding pages to convert audience into evidence.** Add GitHub Sponsors and a clear “hardware fund” budget goal; publish what a loan enables and post results back. Sponsors are more likely to respond when there is a repeatable test plan and public deliverable than an unbounded hardware request.
