# Strim logo exploration — research notes

Reviewed 29 October 2026. These are visual observations of official product or brand pages, not user testing. The references span local model tools, developer utilities, and Mac apps. Some brands use gradients, illustration, or richer palettes; those are useful counterexamples, not a color direction for Strim.

## Eight principles for Strim

1. **Own one idea.** Build around a stream-shaped lowercase s; do not combine tokens, prompt cursor, server, chip, and sparkle in one mark.
2. **Make the silhouette do the work.** Use one bold continuous shape or a few deliberate segments with open counters. At 16 px it must still read as s, not ≡, E, or a tiny window.
3. **Design the 1-colour master first.** Require black on white and white on near-black before adding the single indigo accent. Skip gradients, glow, shadows, and extra hues.
4. **Keep symbol and name independent.** Use the mark alone for the favicon, app icon, and menu bar; use a horizontal lockup when “strim” needs to be read.
5. **Use the Mac icon as a container, not a second logo.** One flat neutral field, one centered mark, and generous optical padding; no border or ornamental tile inside the system squircle.
6. **Treat tiny sizes as their own design constraint.** Open the counters, simplify cuts, and keep strokes thick enough that 16 px and an 18 pt template glyph hold their shape.
7. **Let the name carry the character.** Use lowercase geometric letterforms, moderate tracking, and a confident medium-to-semibold weight. Keep the i token as the one custom detail; do not add several cuts and ligatures at once.
8. **Give every mark air.** Define a repeatable clear-space rule and preserve it around standalone symbols and lockups, following the practical usage discipline in Linear, Raycast, MLX, and Open WebUI's brand resources.
