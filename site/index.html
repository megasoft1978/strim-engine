Run Qwen3.8-27B on your Mac like a server.

One Swift binary, built for one model: a local backend for Claude Code and Codex.

brew install megasoft1978/strim/strim
See the benchmarks

Apple Silicon, macOS 14 or newer. Tested only on a 64 GB M5 Max. MIT license.

What it does well

  • 48-50% faster decode

    than rapid-mlx, and 2.1-2.3x the speed of mlx-vlm. At 4K: 70.72 vs 47.75 vs 30.69 tok/s.

  • 2.8-2.9x faster for agents sharing a prefix

    with four clients on one 16K prefix. Median first token 20.71 s vs 75.08 s and 70.93 s.

  • 30-55% lower median first token

    at 4 to 8 concurrent requests, against each rival.

All six measurements, losses included

Where it is not faster. Cold prefill is at or within 2% of the best rival at 4K and 16K, 5% behind it at 32K; aggregate throughput at 4 to 8 concurrent requests trails the best rival by more than 2% in 3 of 6 cells (5-6%); p95 first-token latency is more than 2% worse in 2 of 6 cells (5-7%).

Validity. Preliminary results: 6 of 6 engine sessions were measured while other jobs loaded the machine (highest 1-minute load 11.8); a clean rerun is in progress. Direction matches the earlier interleaved run. Earlier run summary.

FAQ

Why only one model?

Everything is tuned for one dense model: its attention state, its speculative-decoding head and its prefix cache. That is why these numbers are possible, and why Strim will not run your other models. Others are on the roadmap.

Does speculative decoding change the output?

Draft tokens are verified by the full model, but batching can flip near-tie tokens. Greedy output over 8 fixed prompts and 64 tokens: Strim against itself 8/8 identical, against rapid-mlx 7/8, against mlx-vlm 6/8. Quality and exactness.

Does it run on a 32 GB Mac or an M1?

Only a 64 GB M5 Max has been tested. For 32 GB the computed estimate is 114K/214K (low) 154K/291K (high) of context (not measured); 16 GB and below are unsupported. See supported scope.

Why is cold prefill not faster?

On a prompt the engine has never seen, prefill is compute-bound and Strim has no faster kernel than the rivals: cold prefill is at or within 2% of the best rival at 4K and 16K, 5% behind it at 32K; aggregate throughput at 4 to 8 concurrent requests trails the best rival by more than 2% in 3 of 6 cells (5-6%); p95 first-token latency is more than 2% worse in 2 of 6 cells (5-7%). Strim wins by not repeating prefill (exact prefix cache) and by decoding faster.
